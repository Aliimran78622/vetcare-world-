Introduction

From Herefordshire in England comes the red, white-faced breed that stocked the ranches of America, Australia and Argentina.

Herefords are hardy, quiet and efficient — they turn rough grass into good beef and stay in condition where softer breeds struggle.

Varieties & Types of Hereford

The breed is kept in two forms:

  • Horned Hereford — The original form with short, curved horns.
  • Polled Hereford — A naturally hornless strain bred from horned Herefords — in strong demand for easy handling.

Characteristics

  • Deep Body — Deep through the ribs and hindquarter — built for beef.
  • Red Coat — Rich red body with white face, crest and underline.
  • Thick Top Line — Wide, level back carrying the best beef cuts.
  • White Face — The famous white face — the breed's signature.

Strengths

  • Extremely hardy on range
  • Quiet, easy to handle
  • Efficient on rough grass
  • Fertile, regular calving
  • White face marks cross calves

Keep in Mind

The white face passes to crossbred calves — ranchers call them 'baldies'. In strong sun and dusty conditions, watch the unpigmented skin around the eyes for irritation.

Beef Production

Herefords dress out at 58–63% — a 600 kg steer gives roughly 350–380 kg of carcass.

Their real profit is efficiency: quality beef produced from grass alone, at low cost, in hard country.

Price & Buying Tips

Typical price — international (indicative): US $1,200 – 3,000 — per breeding animal; pedigree bulls cost more. All prices are indicative and move with the market.

Prices vary by country, pedigree and meat-market demand. Commercial females sit at the lower end of the range; the polled form is in strong demand.

Before You Buy — Checklist

  • Body frame & muscling
  • Legs & hooves sound
  • Breeding organs normal
  • Teeth & age checked
  • Body condition score 3+
  • Vaccination & deworming record
  • Temperament calm
  • Weight on scale, not by eye

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do Herefords have white faces?

It is the breed's fixed trademark — every pure Hereford is born with a white face, and the mark passes to its crossbred calves, which ranchers call 'baldies'.

Are Herefords good for grass farming?

Yes — they are one of the most efficient breeds at turning rough grass into beef, and they stay hardy in tough conditions.

Hereford or Angus — which is better?

Both are top beef breeds. Angus is famous for marbling and is polled; Hereford is famously hardy, quiet and efficient on grass. Many herds cross the two.

What is the price of a Hereford animal?

About US $1,200–3,000 per breeding animal (indicative); pedigree bulls cost more.
