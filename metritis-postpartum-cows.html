Postpartum Metritis in Dairy Cows

Global Livestock Health & Veterinary Learning

Home › Disease & Treatment › Postpartum Metritis in Dairy Cows

Veterinary Education · Cattle & Buffalo Health

Postpartum metritis is inflammation and infection of the uterus after calving, often associated with retained placenta, difficult calving, twins or poor hygiene. Severe cases can cause systemic illness.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

  1. 1
    Foul-smelling watery uterine discharge(Watery discharge with a foul smell after calving)
  2. 2
    Fever(High body temperature)
  3. 3
    Reduced appetite(Cow eats less or refuses feed)
  4. 4
    Depression(Dull, listless and withdrawn)
  5. 5
    Reduced milk yield(Milk production drops)
  6. 6
    Dehydration(Dry muzzle and sunken eyes)

Causes and Transmission

Bacteria enter the uterus after calving. Risk rises with dystocia, retained fetal membranes, stillbirth, poor body condition and contaminated calving environments.

Pathogenesis

After calving, the uterus is open to bacteria from the environment, and contamination is normal — but when the cow's defences are weakened (difficult calving, retained membranes, twins, milk fever, poor hygiene), bacteria multiply in the uterus instead of being cleared.

The infected uterine wall becomes inflamed and releases toxins into the bloodstream, causing fever, depression and reduced appetite. The uterus fills with foul-smelling discharge, and the cow may stop eating and drop in milk; severe cases progress to toxaemia and peritonitis.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Diagnosis uses calving history, temperature, general condition, vaginal discharge and reproductive examination. The veterinarian distinguishes metritis from normal postpartum discharge and other reproductive disorders.

Differential Diagnosis

Several conditions can look similar. A veterinarian distinguishes them by the signs, history and laboratory findings:

  • Retained foetal membranes — show membranes hanging from the vulva and are often the cause of metritis.
  • Milk fever — causes a down cow after calving without fever or discharge.
  • Ketosis — causes reduced appetite and a milk drop without fever or foul discharge.
  • Peritonitis — causes severe abdominal pain and a grunt, sometimes after uterine rupture.

Treatment and Case Management

Systemically ill cows require prompt veterinary treatment. Antimicrobials, anti-inflammatory medicines and fluids may be considered according to clinical status and local guidelines. Do not insert unapproved products or attempt intrauterine treatment without veterinary advice.

When to seek urgent help: Fever, foul discharge, severe depression or refusal to eat after calving requires prompt veterinary attention.

Recommended Medicines

  1. 1
    Antibiotic – Oxytetracycline(Treats the uterine bacterial infection)10 mg/kg IM daily (conventional) — or LA preparation: 20 mg/kg IM/SC once, repeat after 3–4 days if needed
  2. 2
    NSAID – Flunixin meglumine(Reduces fever, pain and inflammation)2.2 mg/kg IV once daily
  3. 3
    Fluid therapy – IV fluids(Corrects dehydration in a systemically ill cow)As prescribed by a veterinarian
⚠ Important Tips:
  • Use antibiotics only under veterinary advice.
  • Never insert unapproved products into the uterus.
  • Fever with foul discharge after calving needs a vet promptly.
  • Observe milk and meat withdrawal periods after treatment.

Prevention and Herd Management

Provide clean calving areas, assist difficult births hygienically, monitor cows after calving, manage body condition and promptly investigate retained placenta or fever.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the symptoms of metritis in cows after calving?

Foul-smelling watery uterine discharge, fever, reduced appetite, depression, reduced milk yield and dehydration. Some cows have uterine infection without obvious systemic illness.

What causes postpartum metritis in dairy cows?

Bacteria enter the uterus after calving. Risk rises with dystocia, retained fetal membranes, stillbirth, poor body condition and contaminated calving environments.

How is metritis in cows diagnosed?

Diagnosis uses calving history, temperature, general condition, vaginal discharge and reproductive examination. The veterinarian distinguishes metritis from normal postpartum discharge and other reproductive disorders.

How is postpartum metritis treated?

Systemically ill cows require prompt veterinary treatment. Antimicrobials, anti-inflammatory medicines and fluids may be considered according to clinical status and local guidelines. Do not insert unapproved products or attempt intrauterine treatment without veterinary advice.

How can metritis after calving be prevented?

Provide clean calving areas, assist difficult births hygienically, monitor cows after calving, manage body condition and promptly investigate retained placenta or fever.

Why does my cow have smelly discharge after calving?

Foul-smelling watery uterine discharge after calving, often with fever and reduced appetite, is typical of postpartum metritis. Systemically ill cows require prompt veterinary treatment, so contact a veterinarian.

How to treat metritis in cows?

Systemically ill cows require prompt veterinary treatment, and antimicrobials, anti-inflammatory medicines and fluids may be considered according to clinical status and local guidelines. Do not insert unapproved products or attempt intrauterine treatment without veterinary advice.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
