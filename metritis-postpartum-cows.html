Postpartum Metritis in Dairy Cows

Postpartum metritis is inflammation and infection of the uterus after calving, often associated with retained placenta, difficult calving, twins or poor hygiene. Severe cases can cause systemic illness.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

Foul-smelling watery uterine discharge, fever, reduced appetite, depression, reduced milk yield and dehydration. Some cows have uterine infection without obvious systemic illness.

Causes and Transmission

Bacteria enter the uterus after calving. Risk rises with dystocia, retained fetal membranes, stillbirth, poor body condition and contaminated calving environments.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Diagnosis uses calving history, temperature, general condition, vaginal discharge and reproductive examination. The veterinarian distinguishes metritis from normal postpartum discharge and other reproductive disorders.

Treatment and Case Management

Systemically ill cows require prompt veterinary treatment. Antimicrobials, anti-inflammatory medicines and fluids may be considered according to clinical status and local guidelines. Do not insert unapproved products or attempt intrauterine treatment without veterinary advice.

When to seek urgent help: Fever, foul discharge, severe depression or refusal to eat after calving requires prompt veterinary attention.

Prevention and Herd Management

Provide clean calving areas, assist difficult births hygienically, monitor cows after calving, manage body condition and promptly investigate retained placenta or fever.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
