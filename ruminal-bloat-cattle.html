Ruminal Bloat in Cattle

Ruminal bloat occurs when gas accumulates in the rumen and cannot be expelled normally. It may be frothy or free-gas bloat and can become life-threatening by compressing the lungs and circulation.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

Distended left flank, discomfort, repeated lying down and rising, reduced rumination, rapid breathing and severe respiratory distress in advanced cases.

Causes and Transmission

Frothy bloat may follow lush legume pasture or finely ground, rapidly fermentable feed. Free-gas bloat can occur with oesophageal obstruction or impaired eructation.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Diagnosis is usually based on rapid onset, left-sided abdominal distension and examination. The veterinarian distinguishes frothy from free-gas bloat and checks for obstruction or other causes.

Treatment and Case Management

Bloat is an emergency when distension is severe or breathing is affected. Immediate veterinary intervention may be required, including appropriate decompression or anti-foaming treatment. Do not force liquids into an animal that is struggling to breathe or swallow.

When to seek urgent help: Severe left-sided distension or breathing difficulty is an immediate emergency. Contact a veterinarian without delay.

Prevention and Herd Management

Introduce lush pasture and concentrate gradually, provide adequate roughage, avoid sudden diet changes, monitor high-risk feeds and use preventive products only on veterinary or label advice.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
