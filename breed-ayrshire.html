Introduction

The Ayrshire was bred in south-west Scotland for farms where grass is rough and weather is cold and wet.

She is famous for her red-and-white coat, upturned horns and one of the best-shaped udders of any dairy breed — a true grazing cow.

Ayrshire at a Glance

OriginAyrshire, south-west Scotland
TypeDairy breed (grazing)
Milk per lactation5,000–7,500 kg (305 days)
Milk fatAbout 4.0%
CoatSharp red or mahogany patches on clean white
Price (indicative)US $1,200–2,500 per breeding cow

Characteristics

  • Legs & Feet — Strong legs made for walking and grazing hills.
  • Red & White Coat — Sharp red or mahogany patches on clean white.
  • Lyre Horns — Long horns curving up and out — often removed in herds.
  • The Face — Alert, bright-eyed and active in nature.

Strengths

  • Excellent grazing ability
  • Best-shaped udder of dairy breeds
  • Hardy in cold & wet weather
  • Active, healthy feet and legs
  • Good milk fat around 4.0%

Keep in Mind

Ayrshires are rare in Pakistan — mostly seen in crosses. They are active cattle and need confident handling, and they were bred for cool grazing country, not desert heat.

Milk & Meat Production

An Ayrshire gives 5,000–7,500 kg of milk per lactation with about 4.0% butterfat — steady milk that excels where feed comes mostly from grazing.

The breed is a show-ring favourite, and its well-shaped, strongly attached udder is the model other dairy breeds are judged against.

Price & Buying Tips

Typical price — international (indicative): US $1,200 – 2,500 — per breeding cow, depending on records & pregnancy. All prices are indicative and move with the market.

Prices vary by country, milk record and pregnancy status. The breed is strong in Finland and Canada; being less common, prices vary widely.

Before You Buy — Checklist

  • Milk record of the cow & mother
  • Udder — 4 working quarters
  • Teats — no lumps or sores
  • Hooves & legs sound
  • Body condition score 2.75–3.25
  • Vaccination & deworming record
  • Pregnancy status confirmed
  • Milk let-down tested

Frequently Asked Questions

How much milk does an Ayrshire give?

About 5,000–7,500 kg per 305-day lactation with around 4.0% butterfat.

What is special about the Ayrshire?

Her red-and-white coat, hardiness on rough grazing, and what many judges call the best-shaped udder of any dairy breed.

Is the Ayrshire a grazing breed?

Yes — she was bred for grass-based farms in cold, wet Scotland and thrives on pasture.

What is the price of an Ayrshire cow?

About US $1,200–2,500 per breeding cow internationally (indicative).
