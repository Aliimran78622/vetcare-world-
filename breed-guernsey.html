Channel Islands • Dairy Breed • Famous for Golden-Coloured Milk

Introduction

The Guernsey comes from the Channel Island of the same name, off the coast of France — a fawn-and-white cow with a secret in her milk.

Her milk is naturally golden from beta-carotene — the same goodness found in carrots. It is rich, tasty and prized for drinking and butter.

Characteristics

  • The Udder — Well shaped and strongly attached for her size.
  • Fawn & White Coat — Warm fawn patches on white, with a white face blaze.
  • Medium Frame — Smaller than a Holstein, bigger than a Jersey.
  • Gentle Head — Quiet, feminine head with a slightly dished face.

Strengths

  • Naturally golden, rich milk
  • Gentle and easy to handle
  • Good butterfat 4.5–5.0%
  • Efficient grazer
  • Early maturing heifers

Keep in Mind

Guernseys are rare in Pakistan and yield less volume than a Holstein — keep them for milk quality, not maximum litres.

Milk & Meat Production

A Guernsey gives 5,000–7,000 kg of milk per lactation, rich milk with fat about 4.5–5.0%. The golden colour comes from natural beta-carotene in the milk.

The milk is prized for drinking, and it makes deep golden butter and cream. Heifers mature early, so they join the milking herd sooner.

Price & Buying Tips

Typical price — international (indicative): US $1,200 – 2,500 — per breeding cow, depending on records & pregnancy. All prices are indicative and move with the market.

Prices vary by country, milk record and pregnancy status. The breed is mostly kept in the UK, USA and Channel Islands, and is prized for its golden milk.

Before You Buy — Checklist

  • Milk record of the cow & mother
  • Udder — 4 working quarters
  • Teats — no lumps or sores
  • Hooves & legs sound
  • Body condition score 2.75–3.25
  • Vaccination & deworming record
  • Pregnancy status confirmed
  • Milk let-down tested

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Guernsey milk golden?

It is naturally rich in beta-carotene — the same pigment that makes carrots orange — which gives the milk and butter a golden colour.

How much milk does a Guernsey give?

About 5,000–7,000 kg per lactation, with 4.5–5.0% butterfat.

Guernsey or Jersey — which is better?

Both give rich milk. The Jersey is smaller with slightly higher fat; the Guernsey is a little bigger with famously golden milk. Choice depends on your market.

What is the price of a Guernsey cow?

About US $1,200–2,500 per breeding cow internationally (indicative).
