Bovine Tuberculosis (bTB)

Global Livestock Health & Veterinary Learning

Home › Disease & Treatment › Bovine Tuberculosis (bTB)

Veterinary Education · Cattle & Buffalo Health

Bovine tuberculosis is a chronic infectious disease caused mainly by Mycobacterium bovis. It affects cattle and can infect other animals and people.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

  1. 1
    Chronic Weight Loss(Slow, steady loss of body condition)
  2. 2
    Reduced Productivity(Lower milk yield and performance)
  3. 3
    Persistent Cough(Cough that does not go away)
  4. 4
    Enlarged Lymph Nodes(Swollen glands under the skin)
  5. 5
    Respiratory Difficulty(Laboured or difficult breathing)

Causes and Transmission

The organism spreads mainly through inhalation of infectious droplets and can also spread through contaminated milk and close contact. Infection may remain unnoticed for long periods.

Pathogenesis

Mycobacterium bovis is usually inhaled in droplets from an infected animal, or ingested in contaminated milk or feed. The bacteria are taken up by macrophages in the lungs or gut but resist being killed inside them, and the immune system walls them off into granulomas (tubercles).

Over months the tubercles enlarge, may break down and spread through the lymph nodes and bloodstream to other organs. Chronic infection causes progressive weight loss and weakness and, when the lungs are involved, a chronic cough.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Diagnosis relies on official tuberculin skin testing, interferon-gamma testing and confirmatory laboratory methods as directed by the competent authority. A normal appearance does not rule out infection.

Differential Diagnosis

Several conditions can look similar. A veterinarian distinguishes them by the signs, history and laboratory findings:

  • Johne's disease — causes chronic wasting and diarrhoea in adult cattle without respiratory signs.
  • Chronic pneumonia and lung abscesses — cause a chronic cough but not the progressive wasting pattern of tuberculosis.
  • Liver fluke — causes chronic weight loss and anaemia, usually with bottle jaw and without a cough.
  • Bovine leucosis — can cause lymph node enlargement and wasting, but without tubercles in the lungs.

Treatment and Case Management

Treatment of infected cattle is generally not used in official eradication programmes. Follow testing, reporting, movement restrictions and removal instructions from veterinary authorities.

When to seek urgent help: Suspected bovine TB should be handled through the official veterinary disease-control system; do not sell or move suspect animals without advice.
No specific medicine: There is no routine curative treatment for bovine tuberculosis in cattle. Follow official testing, reporting, movement-control and removal instructions from the veterinary authority.
⚠ Important Tips:
  • Report suspected cases to the veterinary authority.
  • Do not sell or move suspect animals without veterinary advice.
  • Handle suspected cases only through the official veterinary disease-control system.
  • Do not drink raw milk from suspect animals.

Prevention and Herd Management

Buy animals from monitored herds, maintain testing and traceability, avoid raw milk, improve ventilation and follow official herd-level control programmes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the symptoms of bovine tuberculosis in cattle?

Chronic weight loss, reduced productivity, a persistent cough, enlarged lymph nodes and sometimes respiratory difficulty. Many infected animals show no obvious signs early in infection.

How does bovine TB spread between cattle?

The organism spreads mainly through inhalation of infectious droplets, and can also spread through contaminated milk and close contact. Infection may remain unnoticed for long periods.

How is bovine tuberculosis diagnosed?

Diagnosis relies on official tuberculin skin testing, interferon-gamma testing and confirmatory laboratory methods as directed by the competent authority. A normal appearance does not rule out infection.

Can bovine TB in cattle be treated?

Treatment of infected cattle is generally not used in official eradication programmes. Follow testing, reporting, movement restrictions and removal instructions from the veterinary authorities.

Can bovine tuberculosis spread to people?

Yes, bovine tuberculosis can infect people as well as cattle. Avoid raw milk, and follow official herd-level control programmes. Suspected cases should be handled through the official veterinary disease-control system.

Is TB in cattle curable?

Treatment of infected cattle is generally not used in official eradication programmes. Infected herds must follow testing, reporting, movement restrictions and removal instructions from the veterinary authorities.

Can humans get TB from cows?

Yes, bovine tuberculosis can infect people. Avoid raw milk, follow official herd-level control programmes, and handle suspected cases through the official veterinary disease-control system.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
