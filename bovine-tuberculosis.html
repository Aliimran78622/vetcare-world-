Bovine Tuberculosis (bTB)

Bovine tuberculosis is a chronic infectious disease caused mainly by Mycobacterium bovis. It affects cattle and can infect other animals and people.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

Chronic weight loss, reduced productivity, persistent cough, enlarged lymph nodes and sometimes respiratory difficulty. Many infected animals show no obvious signs early in infection.

Causes and Transmission

The organism spreads mainly through inhalation of infectious droplets and can also spread through contaminated milk and close contact. Infection may remain unnoticed for long periods.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Diagnosis relies on official tuberculin skin testing, interferon-gamma testing and confirmatory laboratory methods as directed by the competent authority. A normal appearance does not rule out infection.

Treatment and Case Management

Treatment of infected cattle is generally not used in official eradication programmes. Follow testing, reporting, movement restrictions and removal instructions from veterinary authorities.

When to seek urgent help: Suspected bovine TB should be handled through the official veterinary disease-control system; do not sell or move suspect animals without advice.

Prevention and Herd Management

Buy animals from monitored herds, maintain testing and traceability, avoid raw milk, improve ventilation and follow official herd-level control programmes.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
