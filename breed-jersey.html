Introduction

From the small island of Jersey in the English Channel comes the world's favourite butterfat breed — small, fawn and gentle.

Jersey milk is rich and creamy, ideal for butter, ghee and cheese. The cow eats less than a Holstein, so she suits small farms and hot climates well.

Jersey at a Glance

TypeDairy breed

Butterfat4.8–5.5% — the highest of the dairy breeds
Cow weight400–480 kg — the smallest major dairy breed
CoatLight fawn to dark brown, pale muzzle ring
Price (indicative)US $1,200–2,500 per breeding cow

Characteristics

  • The Udder — Large for her size, soft and well attached.
  • Fawn Coat — Light fawn to dark brown, with a pale muzzle ring.
  • Small Frame — The smallest of the major dairy breeds.
  • Dished Face — Wide forehead, dished profile, gentle eyes.

Strengths

  • Richest milk — top butterfat
  • Eats less than big breeds
  • Easy calving, fertile
  • Handles heat better than Holstein
  • Long productive life

Keep in Mind

Total milk volume is lower than a Holstein’s — the Jersey wins on richness, not litres. Jersey bulls can be temperamental and should be handled with care.

Milk & Meat Production

A Jersey gives 5,000–7,000 kg of milk per 305-day lactation — less milk than a Holstein, but far richer: fat 4.8–5.5% and protein about 3.7–3.9%.

That rich milk is ideal for butter, ghee and cheese. Calves are small at birth, so calving is easy and rarely needs help.

Price & Buying Tips

Typical price — international (indicative): US $1,200 – 2,500 — per breeding cow, depending on records & pregnancy. All prices are indicative and move with the market.

Prices vary by country, milk record and pregnancy status. Jersey crosses are popular in Pakistan for heat tolerance, and the breed’s lower feed cost makes it economical for small farms.

Before You Buy — Checklist

  • Milk record of the cow & mother
  • Udder — 4 working quarters
  • Teats — no lumps or sores
  • Hooves & legs sound
  • Body condition score 2.75–3.25
  • Vaccination & deworming record
  • Pregnancy status confirmed
  • Milk let-down tested

Frequently Asked Questions

How much milk does a Jersey cow give?

About 5,000–7,000 kg per lactation (305 days), with the richest milk of the dairy breeds — 4.8–5.5% butterfat.

Why is Jersey milk famous?

Its high butterfat and protein make it ideal for butter, ghee and cheese, and give it a rich, creamy taste.

Is the Jersey good for hot climates?

Yes — she handles heat better than a Holstein and eats less, which suits small farms in hot regions like Pakistan.

What is the price of a Jersey cow?

About US $1,200–2,500 per breeding cow internationally (indicative), depending on records and pregnancy.
