Introduction

Aberdeen Angus comes from north-east Scotland. Solid black, naturally hornless and heavily muscled, it changed beef farming forever.

Angus beef is famous worldwide for fine marbling — the white fat flecks inside the meat that make it tender and juicy. Restaurants pay extra for the Angus name.

Varieties & Types of Aberdeen Angus

One breed in two recognised colours:

  • Black Angus — The original and most famous form — solid black, polled and thick-set.
  • Red Angus — Same breed in red — popular in hot climates, as red coats reflect heat better.

Characteristics

  • Deep Muscling — Thick through the back, loin and hindquarter.
  • Solid Black Coat — Black skin and coat — red form also recognised.
  • Strong Top Line — Long, level back carrying valuable cuts of meat.
  • Polled Head — No horns at all — a smooth, rounded poll.

Strengths

  • Premium marbled beef
  • Naturally polled — no horns
  • Easy calving, great mothers
  • Hardy and adaptable
  • Top demand in world markets

Keep in Mind

Angus is a beef breed, not a milker. The black coat absorbs heat, so in very hot climates the Red Angus form or shaded grazing suits better.

Beef Production

Angus cattle dress out at 60–65% — a 600 kg steer gives roughly 360–390 kg of carcass.

The fine marbling inside the meat earns premium prices per kilogram, and Angus is the top crossing sire for beef herds worldwide.

Price & Buying Tips

Typical price — international (indicative): US $1,500 – 4,000 — per breeding animal; pedigree bulls cost much more. All prices are indicative and move with the market.

Prices vary by country, pedigree and meat-market demand. Registered breeding bulls can sell far above this range, and certified Angus beef earns more.

Before You Buy — Checklist

  • Body frame & muscling
  • Legs & hooves sound
  • Breeding organs normal
  • Teeth & age checked
  • Body condition score 3+
  • Vaccination & deworming record
  • Temperament calm
  • Weight on scale, not by eye

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Angus beef so famous?

Fine marbling — small flecks of fat inside the meat — makes Angus beef tender, juicy and full of flavour, so restaurants and shops pay a premium for it.

Do Angus cattle have horns?

No — Angus are naturally polled (born hornless), which makes handling safer and easier.

What is the difference between Black and Red Angus?

Only the coat colour. Red Angus is the same breed and is often preferred in hot climates because red coats reflect heat better.

What is the price of an Angus animal?

About US $1,500–4,000 per breeding animal (indicative); pedigree bulls cost much more.
