Bovine Viral Diarrhea (BVD)

Bovine viral diarrhea is a viral disease complex that can affect the respiratory, digestive and reproductive systems of cattle. Persistently infected animals can be important sources of infection.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

Fever, diarrhoea, oral erosions, respiratory signs, poor growth, reproductive losses and congenital abnormalities. Persistently infected cattle may appear normal while shedding virus.

Causes and Transmission

BVD virus spreads through direct contact, secretions, contaminated equipment and infected pregnant animals. Infection of a fetus at a particular stage of gestation can result in a persistently infected calf.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Testing may include antigen detection, PCR or antibody testing, interpreted with vaccination and herd history. Identifying persistently infected animals is central to control.

Treatment and Case Management

There is no specific antiviral cure. Care is supportive and secondary infections are managed under veterinary direction. Confirmed persistently infected animals are managed according to local herd-health and regulatory guidance.

When to seek urgent help: Severe diarrhoea, dehydration, mouth lesions or abortions across a herd need veterinary investigation.

Prevention and Herd Management

Use biosecurity, test incoming animals, identify and manage persistently infected cattle, avoid sharing contaminated equipment and implement a veterinarian-designed vaccination programme where appropriate.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
