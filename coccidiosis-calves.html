Coccidiosis in Calves

Coccidiosis is an intestinal disease caused by Eimeria parasites. It is most common in young animals under stress or housed in contaminated, crowded conditions.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

Diarrhoea that may contain mucus or blood, straining, dehydration, poor growth, rough coat and weakness. Some infected calves shed oocysts without obvious illness.

Causes and Transmission

Calves ingest infective oocysts from contaminated feed, water, bedding and surfaces. High stocking density, wet pens and mixing age groups increase exposure.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Diagnosis combines age, clinical signs, herd history and faecal examination. Oocyst counts must be interpreted carefully because shedding alone does not prove that coccidia are causing disease.

Treatment and Case Management

Treatment depends on clinical severity and locally approved anticoccidial products. Supportive fluid therapy is important in dehydrated calves. A veterinarian should assess bloody diarrhoea and rule out other causes.

When to seek urgent help: Bloody diarrhoea, severe straining, dehydration or weakness in calves requires prompt veterinary assessment.

Prevention and Herd Management

Keep pens dry and clean, reduce crowding, separate age groups, clean feeding and watering equipment and reduce stress during weaning or transport.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
