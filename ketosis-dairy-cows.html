Ketosis in Dairy Cows

Ketosis is a metabolic disease associated with negative energy balance, most often in early lactation. Cows mobilise body fat and produce excess ketone bodies, which can reduce appetite and milk yield.

This guide covers clinical signs, causes, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Clinical Signs

Reduced appetite, selective refusal of concentrate, reduced milk yield, weight loss, dry faeces and sometimes an unusual smell on breath or milk. Some cases have no obvious signs.

Causes and Transmission

Risk increases with over-conditioned cows at calving, inadequate dry-matter intake, disease around calving and poor transition-period management.

Veterinary Diagnosis

Blood beta-hydroxybutyrate testing is commonly used. Milk or urine ketone tests may help screen cows, but results should be interpreted with clinical findings and stage of lactation.

Treatment and Case Management

Treatment depends on severity and concurrent disease. A veterinarian may recommend energy support and investigate displaced abomasum, metritis, mastitis or other causes of reduced intake. Avoid unsupervised dosing or repeated drenching of weak animals.

When to seek urgent help: A cow that is recumbent, severely depressed or not eating needs prompt veterinary examination.

Prevention and Herd Management

Maintain body condition targets, maximise feed access and palatability, minimise overcrowding, monitor fresh cows and investigate illnesses that reduce feed intake.

Key Takeaway

Early recognition, accurate diagnosis, responsible veterinary care and herd-level prevention are central to managing this condition. Follow current local veterinary authority guidance and medicine labels.
