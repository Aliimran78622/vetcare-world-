Goat Pox in Goats

Global Livestock Health & Veterinary Learning

Goat Health
Veterinary Education

Goat pox is a contagious viral disease that causes fever and characteristic skin nodules in goats. It reduces growth and milk production, damages hides, and can cause serious illness in young animals.

In Pakistan, goat pox is known as بکریوں کی چیچک and occurs in many goat-rearing areas. Worldwide, the disease is found in Africa, the Middle East and Asia, and it is a WOAH-listed disease. Goat pox does not infect people.

On this page: the cause, animals affected, clinical signs, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Cause

Goat pox is caused by goatpox virus, a capripoxvirus closely related to sheeppox virus and lumpy skin disease virus of cattle. It spreads by direct contact with infected animals and their scabs, and through contaminated equipment, bedding and biting insects. Scabs can carry the virus for months in the environment.

Animals Affected

Goats of all breeds are affected, and young animals usually suffer the most severe disease. Some breeds are more susceptible than others. Sheep are affected by the closely related sheeppox virus.

Clinical Signs

  1. 1
    Fever(High body temperature)
  2. 2
    Loss of appetite(Refusing to eat)
  3. 3
    Raised round nodules on the skin(Nodules on the face, ears, neck, udder, inner thighs and tail)
  4. 4
    Nodules develop into pustules, then dry scabs(Pustules drying into scabs)
  5. 5
    Discharge from the eyes and nose(Watery eye and nasal discharge)
  6. 6
    Nodules inside the mouth cause difficulty eating(Mouth nodules make eating painful)
  7. 7
    Nodules in the respiratory tract cause difficulty breathing(Laboured breathing)
  8. 8
    Reduced milk yield(Drop in milk production)
  9. 9
    Weight loss(Losing body condition)

Diagnosis

The typical skin nodules raise suspicion, and laboratory tests (PCR or virus isolation) confirm the diagnosis. Goat pox must be distinguished from orf, dermatophilosis and mange.

Treatment

There is no specific antiviral treatment. Care is supportive: nursing, soft feed, clean water and protection of skin lesions from flies and secondary infection. Antibiotics are only used for diagnosed secondary bacterial complications, under veterinary direction.

Recommended Medicines

  1. 1
    Antibiotic – Oxytetracycline(Only for diagnosed secondary bacterial infection)10–20 mg/kg IM or SC daily (goats)
  2. 2
    NSAID – Meloxicam(For fever and pain)0.5–1 mg/kg IV/SC once (goats)
  3. 3
    Fluid therapy – Oral electrolytes / IV fluids(To replace fluids and support recovery)As prescribed by a veterinarian
  4. 4
    Topical – Topical antiseptic(To protect skin lesions from flies and secondary infection)As prescribed by a veterinarian
⚠ Important Tips:
  • There is no antiviral cure for goat pox — good nursing care is the treatment.
  • Use antibiotics only under veterinary advice, and only for secondary bacterial infection.
  • Protect skin lesions from flies and keep the goat in clean, dry housing.
  • Vaccinate the flock every year where goat pox occurs, and quarantine new animals.

Prevention

  • Vaccinate with goat pox vaccine where available (annual vaccination in endemic areas)
  • Quarantine new animals before adding them to the flock
  • Isolate affected animals and dispose of scabs safely
  • Clean and disinfect housing and equipment
  • Control biting insects
  • Avoid buying animals from affected flocks

Key Takeaway

Vaccination and strict quarantine of new animals are the most effective ways to keep goat pox out of a flock.

Keep herd health records, consult a qualified veterinarian and follow the animal-health authority's current guidance for your region.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the symptoms of goat pox?

Fever and loss of appetite, with raised round nodules on the skin, especially on the face, ears, neck, udder, inner thighs and tail. Nodules may develop into pustules, then dry scabs, and there may be discharge from the eyes and nose.

What causes goat pox?

Goat pox is caused by goatpox virus, a capripoxvirus closely related to sheeppox virus and lumpy skin disease virus of cattle. It spreads by direct contact with infected animals and their scabs, and through contaminated equipment, bedding and biting insects.

Can goat pox spread to people?

No. Goat pox does not infect people.

How is goat pox treated?

There is no specific antiviral treatment. Care is supportive: nursing, soft feed, clean water and protection of skin lesions from flies and secondary infection. Antibiotics are only used for diagnosed secondary bacterial complications, under veterinary direction.

How can goat pox be prevented?

Vaccinate with goat pox vaccine where available, with annual vaccination in endemic areas. Quarantine new animals, isolate affected animals, dispose of scabs safely, and clean and disinfect housing and equipment.

How to treat goat pox?

There is no specific antiviral treatment for goat pox. Care is supportive, with nursing, soft feed, clean water and protection of skin lesions from flies and secondary infection, so consult a veterinarian.

Why does my goat have lumps on skin?

Raised round nodules on the skin, especially on the face, ears, neck and udder, are typical of goat pox. Nodules may develop into pustules, then dry scabs, so a veterinarian should confirm the diagnosis.
