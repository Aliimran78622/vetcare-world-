Goat Pox in Goats

Goat Health
Veterinary Education

Goat pox is a contagious viral disease that causes fever and characteristic skin nodules in goats. It reduces growth and milk production, damages hides, and can cause serious illness in young animals.

In Pakistan, goat pox is known as بکریوں کی چیچک and occurs in many goat-rearing areas. Worldwide, the disease is found in Africa, the Middle East and Asia, and it is a WOAH-listed disease. Goat pox does not infect people.

On this page: the cause, animals affected, clinical signs, diagnosis, treatment principles and prevention.

Cause

Goat pox is caused by goatpox virus, a capripoxvirus closely related to sheeppox virus and lumpy skin disease virus of cattle. It spreads by direct contact with infected animals and their scabs, and through contaminated equipment, bedding and biting insects. Scabs can carry the virus for months in the environment.

Animals Affected

Goats of all breeds are affected, and young animals usually suffer the most severe disease. Some breeds are more susceptible than others. Sheep are affected by the closely related sheeppox virus.

Clinical Signs

  • Fever and loss of appetite
  • Raised round nodules on the skin, especially on the face, ears, neck, udder, inner thighs and tail
  • Nodules may develop into pustules, then dry scabs
  • Discharge from the eyes and nose
  • Nodules inside the mouth or respiratory tract can cause difficulty eating and breathing
  • Reduced milk yield and weight loss

Diagnosis

The typical skin nodules raise suspicion, and laboratory tests (PCR or virus isolation) confirm the diagnosis. Goat pox must be distinguished from orf, dermatophilosis and mange.

Treatment

There is no specific antiviral treatment. Care is supportive: nursing, soft feed, clean water and protection of skin lesions from flies and secondary infection. Antibiotics are only used for diagnosed secondary bacterial complications, under veterinary direction.

Prevention

  • Vaccinate with goat pox vaccine where available (annual vaccination in endemic areas)
  • Quarantine new animals before adding them to the flock
  • Isolate affected animals and dispose of scabs safely
  • Clean and disinfect housing and equipment
  • Control biting insects
  • Avoid buying animals from affected flocks

Key Takeaway

Vaccination and strict quarantine of new animals are the most effective ways to keep goat pox out of a flock.

Keep herd health records, consult a qualified veterinarian and follow the animal-health authority's current guidance for your region.
